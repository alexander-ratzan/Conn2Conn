# The pooled map: why one shared map translates FC between conditions best (E4)

Companion to `cross_condition_translation.md` (results: section E4.3–E4.4; tables `tables/flows/k256/summary.md`;
figures `figures/flows/k256/`). Code: `run_pooled_ridge.py` (closed form), `fm_common.py` `FMshared` (flow).

## 1. The idea

We predict a subject's FC in a target condition (e.g. working memory) from their FC in a source condition (e.g. rest).
There are 8 FC conditions, hence 56 ordered source → target pairs.

- **Per-pair models** (ridge, CCA decoders, the first flows) learn a separate map for each pair, from 659 training
  subjects.
- **The pooled map** is a single map used for every pair, fit on all 56 pairs at once.

The pooled map wins by a wide margin, and it is the first model to beat simply copying the source pattern. The reason
is that a single map cannot specialise to any one target, so it learns the part of a subject's connectivity that is
shared by all conditions: the subject's fingerprint. It keeps that part of the source and suppresses what is specific
to the source condition. Per-pair maps try to also predict what is specific to the target, and at this sample size that
mostly fits noise.

## 2. Setup

Each subject $i$ and condition $m$ has a code vector $z_i^m \in \mathbb{R}^{k}$, $k = 256$: E2's pooled block-PCA
codes (one basis for all FC conditions, equal-variance block scaling, D2), with each condition centred on its own train
mean. So $z_i^m$ is subject $i$'s deviation from condition $m$'s average connectome, and codes from different conditions
are comparable coordinate by coordinate. Row-stacking the $n = 659$ training subjects gives
$Z_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}$.

A prediction $\hat z_i^t$ is decoded to edges (target basis plus the target's train mean) and scored on held-out
subjects: demeaned $r$, average rank, top-1 identification.

## 3. The models

**Copy (B0).** Predict the source itself:

$$
\hat z_i^t = z_i^s
$$

**Per-pair ridge (B1).** One map per pair, fit on the $n$ training subjects:

$$
B_{st} = \arg\min_B \; \lVert Z_t - Z_s B \rVert_F^2 + \alpha \lVert B \rVert_F^2,
\qquad
\hat z_i^t = z_i^s B_{st}
$$

There are 56 maps of $k^2 = 65{,}536$ coefficients, each from 659 rows.

**Pooled ridge (B1-pooled).** One map for all pairs: the 56 pairs' training rows are stacked into
$X, Y \in \mathbb{R}^{56n \times k}$ (36,904 rows) and

$$
B = \arg\min_B \sum_{s \neq t} \lVert Z_t - Z_s B \rVert_F^2 + \alpha \lVert B \rVert_F^2,
\qquad
\hat z_i^t = z_i^s B \quad \text{for every target } t
$$

The penalty is chosen on validation average rank (D9) and makes no difference: validation scores are identical from
$\alpha = 0$ to $10^{-3}$, so in practice this is least squares. A variant with one map per target (B1-pooled-tgt) pools
only over the 7 sources of each target.

**Shared-map linear flow (`fm_shared`).** The same sharing inside a flow-matching model (E2.1's linear velocity
field). Samples are generated by integrating, from noise $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, T^2 I)$ at $\tau = 0$ to $\tau = 1$, the
velocity

$$
v_\theta(x, \tau \mid z^s, s, t) = \sum_{j=1}^{6} \phi_j(\tau)\,\big( x A_j + z^s B_j + c_j[t, s] \big),
$$

with a fixed time basis $\phi_j$ (polynomial and rational terms, the form of the exact velocity for linear-Gaussian
data). The matrices $A_j, B_j$ are shared by every pair; only the bias $c_j[t, s]$ knows the pair. Training uses
flow matching: with $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, $\tau \sim U(0, 1)$ and $x_\tau = (1 - \tau)\varepsilon + \tau z^t$,

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\,\big\lVert v_\theta(x_\tau, \tau \mid z^s, s, t) - (z^t - \varepsilon) \big\rVert^2 .
$$

The point prediction is the mean of 256 samples at $T = 0.25$. The model class is linear-Gaussian, the same as the pooled
ridge plus a residual covariance; flow matching is a different (stochastic) way of fitting it.

## 4. What the pooled map computes

Split each subject's codes into a fingerprint shared across conditions and a condition-specific deviation:

$$
z_i^m = f_i + d_i^m,
\qquad
f_i = \frac{1}{8} \sum_{m} z_i^m,
\qquad
\sum_m d_i^m = 0 .
$$

Let $S_f = \sum_i f_i^\top f_i$ (how much fingerprints vary across subjects) and
$S_d = \sum_i \sum_m d_i^{m\top} d_i^m$ (how much a subject varies across conditions). Each source appears in 7 pairs,
and the 7 targets of a source sum to "all 8 minus the source". With these two facts, the least-squares solution
($\alpha = 0$) reduces to

$$
B \;=\; \tfrac{8}{7}\, W \;-\; \tfrac{1}{7}\, I,
\qquad
W = \big(8 S_f + S_d\big)^{-1}\, 8 S_f .
$$

$W$ is a filter that acts separately along directions in code space. Along a direction where the fingerprint variance
is $\sigma_f^2$ and the across-condition variance is $\sigma_d^2$, it multiplies by

$$
w = \frac{8\,\sigma_f^2}{8\,\sigma_f^2 + \sigma_d^2} \in [0, 1] .
$$

| The direction is mostly… | $w$ | the pooled map multiplies the source by |
|---|---|---|
| fingerprint (the same in every condition) | $\approx 1$ | $\approx 1$: keep (copy) |
| mixed | between | shrink |
| condition-specific | $\approx 0$ | $-1/7$: slightly reverse (the other conditions' deviations sum to minus the source's) |

So the pooled map is a fingerprint estimator. These directions are those of a Fisher discriminant with subjects as
classes and conditions as repeated measures, which is why identification (average rank, top-1) gains most. The
prediction is the same for every target condition: the model says nothing about what is specific to the target.

This expression matches the fitted least-squares map to $10^{-15}$ in every seed (checked in E6,
`../fingerprint_embedding/fe_common.py` `pooled_map`).

## 5. The same map without regression: a generalized eigendecomposition

**Concept.** Section 4 shows that $B$ depends on the data only through two $k \times k$ scatter matrices: $S_f$
(how subjects' fingerprints differ from each other) and $S_d$ (how a subject's conditions differ from their own
fingerprint). So the regression over 36,904 stacked rows is not needed. One can find the directions in code space
ordered by how much more they vary *between* subjects than *within* a subject across conditions, and weight each
direction by that ratio. This is a generalized eigendecomposition, the same one as Fisher's linear discriminant
analysis (LDA) with subjects as the classes and the 8 conditions as repeated observations of each class. The pooled
map is a soft, reliability-weighted version of an LDA projection: LDA keeps the top few directions with equal weight,
the pooled map keeps every direction, scaled by how fingerprint-like it is.

**The eigenproblem.** Let $M = 8$ conditions and $C = M S_f + S_d$ (the total scatter of all $nM$ code vectors around
zero). Solve the symmetric-definite generalized eigenproblem

$$
M S_f\, v_j \;=\; w_j\, C\, v_j,
\qquad
v_j^\top C\, v_j = 1,
\qquad j = 1, \dots, k .
$$

Each eigenvalue is the fingerprint share of its direction,

$$
w_j \;=\; \frac{v_j^\top (M S_f)\, v_j}{v_j^\top (M S_f + S_d)\, v_j} \;\in\; [0, 1],
$$

the fraction of the variance along $v_j$ that is shared by all of a subject's conditions. Collect the eigenvectors as
columns of $V$ and the eigenvalues in $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(w_1, \dots, w_k)$. The normalisation gives
$V^{-1} = V^\top C$, and the filter of section 4 is $W = C^{-1} M S_f = V \Lambda V^{-1}$. Hence

$$
B \;=\; V\, \mathrm{diag}\big(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_k\big)\, V^{-1},
\qquad
\beta_j \;=\; \frac{M w_j - 1}{M - 1} \;=\; \frac{8 w_j - 1}{7} .
$$

**Applying it** to a subject's code vector $z$ (a row vector) takes three steps:

$$
a_j = z\, v_j \;\; \text{(score along direction } j\text{)},
\qquad
a_j \mapsto \beta_j\, a_j \;\; \text{(reweight)},
\qquad
\hat z = \sum_j \beta_j\, a_j\, u_j \;\; \text{(map back; } u_j \text{ = row } j \text{ of } V^{-1}\text{)} .
$$

A direction with $w_j = 1$ is copied unchanged ($\beta_j = 1$); one with $w_j = 1/8$ is removed ($\beta_j = 0$); one with
$w_j = 0$ is slightly reversed ($\beta_j = -1/7$). In E6, about 56 of the 256 directions have $w_j > 0.5$
(`../fingerprint_embedding/figures/fig_w_spectrum.png`).

**Link to Fisher's discriminant.** With subjects as classes, the between-class scatter is $S_B = M S_f$ and the
within-class scatter is $S_W = S_d$. Fisher's criterion along $v$ is $\lambda = v^\top S_B v / v^\top S_W v$, and the
two eigenproblems share their eigenvectors, with

$$
w_j = \frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_j + 1} .
$$

Equivalently, with $\sigma_b^2$ the between-subject variance of the fingerprints and $\sigma_w^2$ the per-condition
within-subject variance along a direction, $w = \sigma_b^2 / (\sigma_b^2 + \sigma_w^2)$: an intraclass-correlation-like
reliability across conditions.

**Why it equals the regression** (for any number of conditions $M$, every subject in every condition, no penalty). In
the stacked regression each source condition appears in $M - 1$ pairs, and the $M - 1$ targets of a source sum to
$M\bar Z - Z_s$, where $\bar Z$ holds the subjects' fingerprints. Because the deviations sum to zero over conditions,
$\sum_s Z_s^\top Z_s = C$ and $\sum_s Z_s^\top \bar Z = M S_f$, so the normal equations give

$$
X^\top X = (M - 1)\, C,
\qquad
X^\top Y = M^2 S_f - C,
\qquad
B = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top Y = \frac{M}{M - 1}\, C^{-1} M S_f - \frac{1}{M - 1}\, I ,
$$

which is $\tfrac{M}{M-1} W - \tfrac{1}{M-1} I$, i.e. the eigen form above.

**Recipe (no regression, no penalty):**

1. On the train subjects, compute each subject's fingerprint $f_i$ (mean code over the $M$ conditions), then
   $S_f = \sum_i f_i^\top f_i$ and $S_d = \sum_i \sum_m (z_i^m - f_i)^\top (z_i^m - f_i)$.
2. Solve $M S_f v = w\,(M S_f + S_d)\, v$ (one $k \times k$ generalized eigendecomposition, e.g. `scipy.linalg.eigh`).
3. Form $B = V \,\mathrm{diag}\big((M w - 1)/(M - 1)\big)\, V^\top C$.
4. Embed any code vector: $z B$. E6's fingerprint embedding is $f_i B$, the mean fingerprint passed through the filter
   (256-dimensional, effectively spanned by the high-$w$ directions).

**What this buys.** Fitting needs two scatter matrices and one eigendecomposition instead of a 36,904-row regression.
The spectrum $w_j$ is a direct diagnostic of how much of FC is subject-stable. Applying the map needs only one code
vector, so a subject with a single scan can be embedded (fitting it needs subjects with every condition). Natural
variants are a hard LDA truncation (keep the top $q$ directions) or a different weighting of $w_j$, not yet tested.

## 6. Results (test, 5 seeds, the 56 single-source cells without SC)

| Model | Maps | Average rank | Top-1 | Demeaned $r$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge per pair (B1) | 56 | 0.784 | 0.11 | 0.099 |
| Nonlinear flow, per pair (FM-nl) | 56 (shared trunk) | 0.795 | 0.11 | 0.097 |
| Multiview CCA (B2) | 8 encoders, 32-d latent | 0.815 | 0.12 | 0.105 |
| Copy, edges (B0) | – | 0.843 | 0.25 | 0.107 |
| Ridge, one map per target (B1-pooled-tgt) | 8 | 0.843 | 0.18 | 0.119 |
| Shared-map linear flow (`fm_shared`) | 1 | 0.911 | 0.31 | 0.119 |
| **Ridge, one map for all pairs (B1-pooled)** | **1** | **0.916** | **0.32** | **0.124** |

- **Sharing is what matters.** The fewer separate maps a model has, the better it does. The fully pooled map beats the
  copy in 92 % of cells on average rank and 85 % on demeaned $r$; no per-pair model beats it on average rank in
  more than 27 % of cells.
- **Pooling targets matters more than pooling sources.** One map per target (7 sources pooled, one target) gains much
  less than one map for all pairs, whose effective target is the subject's mean over the other 7 conditions, so the
  targets' condition-specific noise averages out.
- **Flow matching adds nothing.** `fm_shared` learns the same map and lands just below the closed form in every seed
  (average rank −0.006, top-1 −0.019).

## 7. Limits

- The pooled map predicts the subject's fingerprint, not anything specific to the target. That is enough for
  identification and for most of the demeaned $r$, but it cannot say how a subject's working-memory FC differs from
  their rest FC.
- Conditions are weighted equally ($S_f$, $S_d$ follow D2's equal-variance block scaling); another weighting changes $w$.
- The derivation assumes every subject has all 8 conditions (true here: matched cohort) and no penalty (the penalty is
  irrelevant here).
