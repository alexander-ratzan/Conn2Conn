\section{Supplementary Material: Sanity Checks and Reproducibility Discipline}\label{supplementary-material-sanity-checks-and-reproducibility-discipline}

This document is a long-form supplement for the Conn2Conn preprint. It combines the compact manuscript supplement with the detailed sanity-check notes from the repository, plus selected machine-readable CSV summaries. The purpose is to make the negative claims auditable: reduction choices, FC measurement noise, nonlinear capacity, richer tractography, family objectives, and operational grid completeness are all checked explicitly.

\section{Supplementary Material}\label{supplementary-material}

This supplement documents the reproducibility and sanity-check discipline behind the main paper. The purpose is not only to provide extra numbers, but to show how the analysis avoids common silent failure modes: population-mean-dominated metrics, split drift, missing grid cells, leakage, dimensionality artifacts, unstable scalar estimators, measurement-noise explanations, and objective mismatch.

\subsection{S2. Metrics and Estimators}\label{s2-metrics-and-estimators}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{S2.1 Why \texttt{demeaned\_pearson} is primary}{S2.1 Why demeaned\_pearson is primary}}\label{s21-why-demeaned_pearson-is-primary}

Raw connectome correlations are dominated by the group-mean connectome. This makes raw Pearson large even when individual deviations are not recovered. The primary reconstruction metric is therefore \texttt{demeaned\_pearson}: subtract the training-set mean target connectome, then compare each subject\textquotesingle s predicted and observed deviation.

The main text leads with \texttt{demeaned\_pearson} and uses fingerprinting metrics (\texttt{avg\_rank}, \texttt{top1\_acc}) as secondary checks. Conventional MSE, R2, and raw Pearson are retained in the grid for diagnostic purposes but should not drive interpretation.

\subsubsection{S2.2 Estimator reporting rule}\label{s22-estimator-reporting-rule}

Every number should be reported with its estimator.

Reconstruction rows include:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  PCA(256)-\textgreater PLS(64)-\textgreater inverse-PCA (\texttt{pca\_pls})
\item
  BayesianRidge on PCA components (\texttt{bayesian\_ridge})
\item
  KernelRidge RBF 3x3 gamma/alpha sweep (\texttt{kernel\_ridge})
\end{itemize}

Downstream scalar rows should be reported with BayesianRidge. The grid includes PCA-\textgreater PLS and KernelRidge scalar rows for completeness, but these are diagnostic-only. A resolved discrepancy showed why: the older report\textquotesingle s cognition baseline and oracle numbers matched BayesianRidge exactly, while an analyst had accidentally read PCA-\textgreater PLS rows. For scalar targets, PCA-\textgreater PLS and KernelRidge produced ill-conditioned behavior, including extremely negative R2 and parcellation-inconsistent results for parcellation-independent inputs.

Missing cells, unexpected non-finite values, and silently absent metrics are treated as failures rather than tolerated as blank table entries.

\subsection{S5. Reliability Ceilings and FC Measurement Noise}\label{s5-reliability-ceilings-and-fc-measurement-noise}

The main question for SC-\textgreater FC is whether the weak prediction reflects true cross-modal independence or merely noisy FC targets. HCP-YA includes repeated FC measurements, allowing an FC-side reliability analysis.

\subsubsection{S5.1 FC is noisy by edge but reliable as a whole connectome}\label{s51-fc-is-noisy-by-edge-but-reliable-as-a-whole-connectome}

At the single-edge level, much of the between-subject variance is noise. Yet the whole connectome is highly identifiable.

\subsubsection{S5.4 Per-subject reliability does not explain SC-\textgreater FC}\label{s54-per-subject-reliability-does-not-explain-sc-fc}

Per-subject FC reliability is heterogeneous, spanning roughly 0 to 0.78. If SC-\textgreater FC were limited by FC measurement noise, cleaner subjects should be more predictable from SC. They are not. A subject\textquotesingle s SC-\textgreater FC quality is uncorrelated with that subject\textquotesingle s FC reliability (Pearson \texttt{r=0.01}, \texttt{p=0.72}). Filtering to high-reliability subjects raises the ceiling but leaves SC-\textgreater FC prediction near 0.08, reducing the fraction captured.

The correct caveat is that this is FC-side evidence. HCP-YA does not provide SC test-retest dMRI, so a uniform SC noise floor cannot be fully ruled out. The supported conclusion is that the SC-\textgreater FC gap is not explained by FC measurement noise or per-subject FC reliability.

\subsection{S7. Richer Structural Representations}\label{s7-richer-structural-representations}

The tractography extension asks whether streamline-count SC is too crude. Named-bundle tractography (\texttt{r2t}) was tested as an alternative structural representation.

The result was negative. r2t features predicted FC worse than count-SC, added no marginal value over SC in marginal tests, and did not lift cognition above the \texttt{bv+demo} floor. This supports the interpretation that the missing cognition signal is not simply hiding in a richer structural feature set, at least in this regime.

\subsection{S9. Mechanism Mode, Confounds, and Localization}\label{s9-mechanism-mode-confounds-and-localization}

The mechanism analysis should be framed as exploratory. The strongest version is not "PC3 is the mechanism"; it is "a property-selected, low-variance, FC-predictable SC mode carries weak family signal." The component index migrates across parcellations: PC3 in Glasser and PC4 in 4S456.

Important safeguards:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  PC1 is strongly confounded with sex and brain volume (R2 about 0.89-0.93) and should not be interpreted as a family mechanism.
\item
  An earlier PC2 signal failed under cross-seed alignment, illustrating why single-seed mechanisms are fragile.
\item
  The mechanism mode has small effects: FC-predictability around R2=0.22-0.24 and sibling AUC around 0.58.
\item
  The spatial localization concentrates in visual and dorsal-attention edges, but the interpretation is hedged.
\end{itemize}

The tractography-reliability check tested whether this localization was simply due to high-strength, short-distance, more reliable tractography edges. Strength and distance explained about 41\% of \texttt{\textbar{}PC3\textbar{}} magnitude, so the confound is real. After partialling both, the visual/DAN enrichment survived:

\subsection{S10. Operational Reproducibility}\label{s10-operational-reproducibility}

The project uses a two-layer design:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Exploratory notebooks discovered the findings and mechanism hypotheses.
\item
  The \texttt{reproduction/} suite imports the same shared setup functions, freezes the splits, reruns confirmatory grids on both parcellations and 10 seeds, writes CSV mirrors, and verifies completeness.
\end{enumerate}

CSV outputs are the source of truth. W\&B is a replay layer, not a dependency. This matters because offline or failed syncs cannot change the analysis.

The operational discipline includes:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Git-synced compute state.
\item
  Frozen split manifests.
\item
  CSV append with provenance.
\item
  Expected-cell manifest before running.
\item
  Verification after merging.
\item
  Leak verdicts separated from scientific outcomes.
\item
  Notebook-vs-grid regression checks for family and mechanism analyses.
\end{itemize}

\section{Sanity-Check Gap Plan}\label{sanity-check-gap-plan}

This file separates checks already closed from existing repository outputs from checks that require new data, saved predictions, or new model runs.

\subsection{Closed With Existing Data}\label{closed-with-existing-data}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Reconstruction quality is decoupled from cognitive lift; see \texttt{supplement\_sanity\_checks.md}, section \texttt{Existing-Data\ Gap\ Closure}.
\item
  The current downstream grid covers three cognition composites plus age/sex controls.
\item
  Seed-level lift bounds show predicted SC alone is near-zero or negative for cognition, while observed FC plus bv+demo is detectably positive.
\item
  Leak verdicts show zero genuine \texttt{LEAK\_FAIL} cells.
\item
  Family AUC shows predicted connectomes can preserve identity/family signal when the representation selects for it.
\item
  Per-subject FC reliability does not explain SC-\textgreater FC achieved performance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Requires New Data Or New Runs}\label{requires-new-data-or-new-runs}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{SC-side reliability/noise}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Repeat dMRI, split-half tractography, bootstrap streamlines, or saved tractography perturbations.
  \item
    Feasibility: medium if raw diffusion/tractography pipeline is available; low from summary CSVs alone.
  \item
    Why it matters: Current repository bounds FC noise well but cannot produce a true SC test-retest ceiling.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Objective interpolation}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: New training grid mixing reconstruction, fingerprint/family, and cognition objectives on frozen splits.
  \item
    Feasibility: medium-high computationally; no new cohort needed.
  \item
    Why it matters: Would turn the objective-mismatch argument from inferential to causal.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Broader phenotype families}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Additional HCP behavioral, personality, motor, emotion, and latent phenotype targets.
  \item
    Feasibility: high if phenotypes are already local; medium if target cleaning is needed.
  \item
    Why it matters: Current grid covers three cognition composites plus age/sex controls, not all behavior.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Predicted-SC calibration/topology}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Saved predicted matrices or regenerated predictions; compare degree, strength, sparsity, modularity, hubs.
  \item
    Feasibility: medium if predictions are cached; medium-low if all predictions must be regenerated.
  \item
    Why it matters: Correlation can look acceptable while graph topology is biologically distorted.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Edge-class stratification}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Per-edge predictions plus distance, network labels, reliability, and streamline-strength bins.
  \item
    Feasibility: medium with saved matrices and atlas metadata.
  \item
    Why it matters: Could show whether useful signal is concentrated in short/long, intra/inter-network, or high-reliability edges.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Split stress tests}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Rerun selected cells under random, family-aware, age/sex-balanced, high-motion-excluded, and low-motion-only splits.
  \item
    Feasibility: medium; compute-heavy but no new data.
  \item
    Why it matters: Would make the split-drift/leakage defense harder to attack.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{External validity}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Second cohort with FC, dMRI-derived SC, demographics, and comparable cognition/behavior targets.
  \item
    Feasibility: low-medium depending on access.
  \item
    Why it matters: HCP-YA-only evidence supports an internal claim, not a universal population claim.
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Full hyperparameter leakage audit}

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Needed: Static/code audit plus small rerun proving PCA, scaling, residualization, and target transforms fit inside folds.
  \item
    Feasibility: high for code audit; medium for rerun.
  \item
    Why it matters: Leak verdicts cover output behavior; this would document every transform boundary.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subparagraph{1. The cross-modal asymmetry is NOT a reliability artifact {[}note{]} (strengthens F1)}\label{1-the-cross-modal-asymmetry-is-not-a-reliability-artifact-note-strengthens-f1}

\paragraph{The honest bottom line on "how much is noise"}\label{the-honest-bottom-line-on-how-much-is-noise}

There isn\textquotesingle t one number, and that\textquotesingle s not a failure to find a straight answer -\/- the question is genuinely \textbf{level-dependent}. The straight answer is the set:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A single FC measurement is \textasciitilde64\% noise} (per-edge individual-difference variance).
\item
  \textbf{Your averaged usable connectome is \textasciitilde41\% noise} (reliability G \textasciitilde{} 0.59).
\item
  \textbf{The reproducible individual signal you can actually predict tops out at demeaned-r 0.49} (the between-session ceiling), and it is \textbf{heterogeneous across people (0 to 0.78)}.
\item
  \textbf{Of that reproducible 0.49, SC explains \textasciitilde17\%, flat across subjects} (independent of each subject\textquotesingle s own reliability).
\end{enumerate}

(Caveat that rides \#4: this is the FC-side accounting; SC\textquotesingle s \emph{own} noise floor is unmeasured -\/- so "SC explains 17\% of reproducible FC" is exact, while "the other 83\% is signal SC doesn\textquotesingle t contain" is the strong interpretation, pending SC test-retest.)

\begin{quote}
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\#\textbf{REVIEW AND QUESTION} -\/- is "ceiling" valid per-subject, or only at the population level?}{\#REVIEW AND QUESTION -\/- is "ceiling" valid per-subject, or only at the population level?}}\label{review-and-question----is-ceiling-valid-per-subject-or-only-at-the-population-level}

\emph{(Flagged for review -\/- logic below seems sound but wants a second look before it hardens any per-subject claim. It does NOT affect the population headline or the flatness result; it sharpens how we\textquotesingle re allowed to phrase the per-subject material.)}

\textbf{The disattenuation logic (Spearman 1904) is a \emph{population} theorem}, not a per-subject law: the correlation between two variables is bounded by the geometric mean of their reliabilities \textbf{in expectation, over a population.} A single subject\textquotesingle s "reliability" (correlation between their two scans) is \textbf{one noisy number from a single pair of measurements} -\/- large standard error. So is their achieved prediction. Comparing two noisy single-subject estimates, some will land achieved \textgreater{} reliability \textbf{purely by sampling noise} -\/- especially low-reliability subjects, whose reliability estimate sits near zero and is easy to exceed by chance. (The -0.002 subject isn\textquotesingle t truly perfectly unpredictable; -0.002 is noise around some small true value, and a noisy achieved score can exceed it.)

\textbf{Precise statement:} the ceiling is a \textbf{valid population bound but an invalid per-subject bound} -\/- per-subject reliability is a single noisy estimate, not a true per-person limit. The theorem holds on average, not pointwise; treating it pointwise produces the contradiction we saw (achieved \textgreater{} "ceiling" for some subjects).

\textbf{Corrected framing (threads both concerns):}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Population level -\/- keep "ceiling".} "Across subjects, individual FC reproduces at mean demeaned-r 0.49; SC-\textgreater FC captures \textasciitilde17\% of that." The disattenuation is legitimate here and is not exceeded on average. -\textgreater{} stays in the main text (bottom-line \#3, E).
\item
  \textbf{Per-subject level -\/- do NOT call it a ceiling.} Call it \textbf{"per-subject reliability"} and describe its \emph{distribution} (0-0.78, rho=0.41, a stable trait). Frame the H result as \textbf{"SC\textquotesingle s prediction is \emph{uncorrelated} with subject reliability"} (a relationship between two measured quantities) -\/- \textbf{not} "SC stays below each subject\textquotesingle s ceiling," which is the framing that breaks.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Net:} the H conclusion stands (flat r=0.01 = SC prediction is reliability-independent), and it\textquotesingle s arguably \emph{cleaner} under this framing. The thing to fix downstream: the per-subject \texttt{fraction\_of\_ceiling} column in \texttt{h\_per\_subject\_achieved\_vs\_ceiling.csv} implicitly treats per-subject reliability as an individual bound -\/- report it as a descriptive ratio at most, and lean on the \textbf{correlation/flatness} statement (and the bv+demo contrast) for the actual claim, not on pointwise "fraction of ceiling."

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\subsubsection{Two ceilings are different objects (and we should report both, labeled)}\label{two-ceilings-are-different-objects-and-we-should-report-both-labeled}

There are \textbf{two distinct ceilings}; conflating them is the deeper source of the tension.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Ceiling A -\/- data / reproducibility ceiling} (\texttt{FC\_day1\ \textless{}-\textgreater{}\ FC\_day2}). \emph{How reproducible is the target itself?} Model-free, a property of the \textbf{data}. "Individual FC only agrees with itself at \textbf{0.49}, so no predictor can exceed the reproducible signal." Valid at population level, breaks per-subject (above).
\item
  \textbf{Ceiling B -\/- model / oracle ceiling} (\texttt{FC-\textgreater{}FC}, \texttt{SC-\textgreater{}SC} through the \emph{same} PCA-\textgreater PLS pipeline). \emph{How well can THIS model class predict the target from a perfect same-modality copy?} A property of \textbf{model + data together}. "Even predicting FC from FC, PCA-\textgreater PLS only reaches X -\/- the architecture has a representational limit." (\texttt{SC-\textgreater{}SC} oracle \textasciitilde{} \textbf{0.647} from earlier work -\/- to recompute consistently in the grid; \texttt{FC-\textgreater{}FC} = \textbf{{[}to compute{]}}.)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ceiling B fixes both earlier complaints:}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Not exceeded per-subject} -\/- it\textquotesingle s a within-modality prediction run through the \emph{same pipeline / estimator / CV / metric} as the cross-modal predictions; an apples-to-apples upper reference, no "two noisy scans" comparison that can flip.
\item
  \textbf{Available for SC} -\/- \texttt{SC-\textgreater{}SC} is just a prediction task, no test-retest needed. The SC-side data gap that blocks Ceiling A \textbf{disappears} for B, so both directions get a consistent ceiling.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{But B is a \emph{looser} bound than A} (the "???" catch): \texttt{FC-\textgreater{}FC} can exploit \textbf{session-specific signal that wouldn\textquotesingle t replicate} in a fresh scan, so B can sit \emph{above} A. B measures "max ability of the model to reproduce \emph{this} connectome," not "max recoverable \emph{individual trait}." \textbf{B cannot substitute for A} for the biological fraction.

\textbf{How to report -\/- a two-rung reference, each labeled for what it bounds:}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{SC-\textgreater FC achieved: 0.085.}
\item
  \textbf{Ceiling B (model oracle), both directions:} \texttt{FC-\textgreater{}FC} = {[}to compute{]}, \texttt{SC-\textgreater{}SC} \textasciitilde{} 0.647 -\textgreater{} "within-modality is the architecture\textquotesingle s best case; cross-modal loses this much." Clean, consistent, available both directions, never exceeded per-subject -\textgreater{} the \textbf{model-capacity / cross-modal-loss} story.
\item
  \textbf{Ceiling A (data reproducibility): 0.49}, FC-side only, heterogeneous -\textgreater{} "of the \emph{reproducible} signal, SC gets \textasciitilde17\%." The \textbf{biological} fraction, with the per-subject caveat + SC-side gap flagged.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Honesty sentence to carry:} \emph{"The within-modality oracle (FC-\textgreater FC) is a model-capacity reference and exceeds the cross-session reproducibility limit, because it can fit session-specific signal that does not replicate; we therefore disattenuate biological claims against the reproducibility ceiling (A) and use the oracle (B) only to quantify cross-modal vs within-modality loss."}
\end{quote}

  \texttt{pearson} fraction \textgreater1.0 is an artifact (raw pearson is dominated by the shared population mean and is uninformative here -\/- use demeaned\_r); not reported as meaningful.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{G + H. Per-subject reliability + does prediction track it?}\label{g--h-per-subject-reliability--does-prediction-track-it}

\textbf{G -\/- per-subject reliability is heterogeneous and a stable trait.} The 0.49 ceiling is a mean; per-subject between-session reliability spans \textasciitilde0 to 0.78 (Glasser: mean 0.489, median 0.494, std \textbf{0.117}, skew \textbf{-0.58}, non-normal p=5e-15), with a low tail (\textasciitilde2\% of subjects \textless{} 0.2; one at -0.002 = pure noise). It\textquotesingle s a stable subject property (within-vs-between per-subject rho=0.41) -\/- consistent with motion/compliance being person-level. (\texttt{outputs/g\_per\_subject\_*}, \texttt{g\_reliability\_hist.png}.)

\textbf{H -\/- SC-\textgreater FC is NOT noise-limited per subject; the gap is uniform independence.}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  A subject\textquotesingle s SC-\textgreater FC prediction quality is \textbf{uncorrelated} with their own FC reliability ceiling: Pearson \textbf{r=0.01 (p=0.72)}, Spearman \textasciitilde0. (bv+demo-\textgreater FC weakly tracks it, r=0.15, p=2e-5.) So cleaner-FC subjects are \textbf{not} more predictable from SC.
\item
  \textbf{Reliability-filtering does not sharpen SC-\textgreater FC} -\/- it makes the fraction \emph{worse}: \textbar{} filter \textbar{} n \textbar{} SC achieved \textbar{} SC ceiling \textbar{} SC fraction \textbar{} \textbar-\/-\/-\textbar-\/-\/-\textbar-\/-\/-\textbar-\/-\/-\textbar-\/-\/-\textbar{} \textbar{} all \textbar{} 857 \textbar{} 0.083 \textbar{} 0.491 \textbar{} \textbf{0.169} \textbar{} \textbar{} drop rel\textless0.2 \textbar{} 841 \textbar{} 0.083 \textbar{} 0.498 \textbar{} 0.167 \textbar{} \textbar{} drop bottom 10\% \textbar{} 771 \textbar{} 0.083 \textbar{} 0.517 \textbar{} 0.160 \textbar{} \textbar{} keep top 50\% \textbar{} 429 \textbar{} 0.080 \textbar{} 0.582 \textbar{} \textbf{0.137} \textbar{} Keeping only high-reliability subjects raises the ceiling (0.49-\textgreater0.58) but SC\textquotesingle s achieved stays flat (\textasciitilde0.08), so the fraction drops. The noisy tail was never the bottleneck.
\item
  (bv+demo-\textgreater FC holds \textasciitilde0.20-0.21 across all filters.)
\item
  \textbf{Why flat r=0.01 is the strong outcome (not a weak/null one):} per-subject achieved prediction is \emph{mechanically} bounded by reliability (you can\textquotesingle t predict noise), so the default expectation was a \textbf{positive} slope by construction. We got flat. SC captures a fixed \textasciitilde0.08 whether a subject\textquotesingle s FC is reliable (0.78) or near-noise (0.0) -\/- which \emph{rules out} the boring ceiling-effect explanation. The filtering result corroborates by going the "wrong" way: dropping unreliable subjects \emph{lowers} the captured fraction (0.169-\textgreater0.137) because the ceiling rises while achieved stays pinned. Ceiling moves, achieved doesn\textquotesingle t = SC has a fixed, modest grip on FC unrelated to FC\textquotesingle s measurement quality.
\item
  \textbf{The bv+demo contrast is what makes it a clean dissociation (not an artifact):} the analysis \emph{does} detect a real reliability effect when one exists -\/- bv+demo-\textgreater FC mildly tracks reliability (r=0.15). That SC\textquotesingle s slope is flat while the baseline\textquotesingle s isn\textquotesingle t means the flatness is not a methodological artifact (it would have hit both).
\item
  \textbf{Conclusion (with the caveat riding it):} the airtight claim is \textbf{the SC-\textgreater FC gap is not FC-measurement-noise and not per-subject reliability} -\/- decisively, at the per-subject level. The reading "SC doesn\textquotesingle t \emph{contain} that part of FC" is the strong \textbf{interpretation}, but is not fully separable from "SC contains it but measures it too noisily, \emph{uniformly} across subjects" -\/- a uniform SC noise floor would also produce a flat line. Distinguishing those needs SC test-retest (data-blocked). So: 95\% of the way to genuine independence; the last 5\% is the SC-reliability hole. Reinforces E.
\item
  (\texttt{outputs/h\_per\_subject\_achieved\_vs\_ceiling.csv}, \texttt{h\_reliability\_filtered\_summary.csv}, \texttt{h\_correlations.csv}, \texttt{h\_achieved\_vs\_ceiling\_scatter.png}.)
\end{itemize}

\section{Closing Interpretation}\label{closing-interpretation}

Taken together, these checks support a narrow but strong conclusion. FC-SC translation is reproducible and directional, but reconstruction accuracy is not a sufficient proxy for downstream cognitive utility. The null is not explained by the PCA reduction axis, obvious nonlinear model capacity, richer tractography features, sample-size trends within the tested regime, FC-side measurement noise, or hard grid leakage. The constructive exception is family signal: predicted connectomes can preserve identity/family information when the objective selects for it, but that does not imply cognition transfer.
